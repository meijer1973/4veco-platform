# Didactic Route Policy

Status: default economics presentation route policy.

Derived from `references/exemplars/1.1.1-golden-presentation/`.

## Default Route

A production economics presentation should normally include:

1. Route contract.
2. Concrete narrative or source anchor.
3. Core concept.
4. Transfer to another context.
5. Misconception control.
6. Canonical procedure or model.
7. Worked example, calculation/setup.
8. Worked example, interpretation/conclusion.
9. Active formative check.
10. Summary and bridge.

Agents may deviate, but they must name the didactic reason.

## Required Slide Roles

- `route_contract`
- `narrative_anchor`
- `concept_definition`
- `concept_transfer`
- `misconception_control`
- `procedure_route`
- `worked_example_calculation`
- `worked_example_interpretation`
- `active_check`
- `summary_bridge`

## Active Check Boundary

A presentation may include a short active-check slide if it checks immediate understanding and does not replace the paragraph's exercise/check surfaces.

An active-check slide may not claim diagnostics, mastery, summative status, automatic sequencing, target-equivalent proof, or completion authority.

## Worked Example Boundary

Calculation/setup and interpretation/conclusion should be separated when the concept has both a numeric step and an economic meaning. Students should see:

- what is chosen;
- what is not chosen;
- what the best non-chosen alternative is;
- how the calculation works;
- what the result means.
