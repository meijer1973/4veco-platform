## 3. Why this paragraph should move

The relocated paragraph answers a different question from immediate output optimisation. The firm first chooses q at a given market price and capacity. The long-run lesson then asks how profit or loss changes the number of firms, market supply and price, under stated cost assumptions. The next monopoly lesson asks what changes when entry is restricted.

This gives each chapter a defensible endpoint:

- **Book 3, Chapter 3.2:** choose and justify the price-taking firm's production and profit in a short-run setting.
- **Book 4, Chapter 4.1:** compare free-entry adjustment with the entry-barrier and demand conditions of monopoly, then complete the monopoly decision procedure.

The transition is thematic. It does not suggest that free competition necessarily develops into monopoly, or that every monopolist earns profit. The existing long-run cost and normal-remuneration assumptions travel with the lesson.

The trade chapter remains complete and continues to provide the conceptual/source-led ending the user selected. Its core targets do not need the formal long-run firm outcome. The one discovered long-run retrieval question must nevertheless be corrected, as specified below.

### Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Main disadvantage in this bounded revision | Judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Move minimum price/production quota to Book 4 | Breaks up the intervention toolkit; its quota comparison supports trade, while a combined agricultural price-support/quota lesson does not fit naturally inside a labour chapter. It also moves an unresolved high-load lesson rather than curing its workload. | Less coherent than the long-run bridge. |
| Move tariff/protectionism | Trade consolidation already uses tariffs and quota receipts. More than one paragraph would need rewriting, and a trade instrument is not automatically a remedy for market failure. | Do not move it. |
| Move a trade or competition mixed paragraph | Leaves a chapter without its independent consolidation before the Book 3 test, or forces that work into an already full lesson/homework. | Do not use consolidation as the funding source. |
| Move the whole trade chapter or reverse the earlier chapter swap | Much larger change than the selected one-lesson adjustment; disturbs the explicitly selected book rhythm. | Outside the minimal option. |
| Keep 15/16 and do not make a transfer | Economically coherent, but does not preserve the desired per-book allocation. | Valid fallback if the cross-book long-run boundary is not accepted. |

These are curriculum-design judgments based on the supplied outlines and current manuscripts, not experimentally established rankings of student outcomes.

Re-use the existing cost models, explanations, figures, independent exercises and 'Korrels voor kwekerijen' target. ## 5. Book-led authority: a scoped transition, not two competing truths

The user's proposed direction is appropriate: **settle the outline; revise the actual student and answer material; then derive the final teacher guide and central curriculum records from that accepted edition**. The present books contain real targets and answers, while the audit found 31 structural placeholders for Books 3–4. Reconstructing the books to satisfy an empty placeholder would reverse the useful direction of work.

The working precedence for this revision should be recorded in one short transition note:

| Question | Working source |
|---|---|
| What scope/sequence is intended? | The owner's selected decisions and the accepted v3 outlines. |
| What existing content should be preserved? | The pinned delivered book manuscripts, source figures, answers and PDFs, except explicitly listed changes. |
| What does a new paragraph actually require students to demonstrate? | Its revised target, supplied source, answer and teaching/practice mapping, checked together. |
| What do teacher guides, blueprints and registry fields need to say? | A faithful account of the accepted outline and the reviewed delivered/revised exercises. |
| What remains binding beyond this design? | Mathematical/economic correctness, applicable external requirements, explicit protected-source decisions and normal publication/merge authority. |

The books are an **operational authoring baseline**, not proof that every printed claim is true. A genuine error can still be corrected. The outline governs deliberate scope; an accidental manuscript omission does not redefine the goal. Conversely, an old placement rule or placeholder does not automatically veto the newly accepted design.

Do not hand-edit the active baseline to imply the migration is already complete. Preserve the current published edition while the revision is in progress. Mark affected old references as pending migration for this scope and keep a single old/new map. Where an existing check assumes an old numeric ID, distinguish that applicability conflict from a content failure; update only the necessary version-aware routing, retain integrity checks, and do not claim a failed check passed.

Before calling the new edition integrated/current, the teacher guide, target records, blueprint, contents, study guides and assessment pointers must agree. Temporary divergence is a work-in-progress state with a defined end, not a second permanent source of truth. Do not alter preserved import manifests to make revised book files appear byte-identical to the original import.

## 6. Timing and review must still begin early

Teacher-guide wording can be written after the student text, but feasibility must be checked while the targets and core questions are being selected. The exercise-builder contract already asks for a whole-lesson equation, not just exercise time.

This proposal resolves one explicitly chosen structural overload. It does **not** establish that all remaining paragraphs fit. The audit's explicit issues remain open at §§3.1.2, 3.1.3, 3.1.5, 4.2.4 and 4.2.5; conditional risks elsewhere also remain. Select actual source/question chains, avoid redundant full recalculation where it supplies no additional required evidence, and keep essential explanations and independent operations. If a route still exceeds the period, report the exact operation/scope trade-off rather than silently buying another lesson or hiding new teaching in homework.

Review the affected targets during revision. Unchanged targets can be reviewed in manageable chapter batches alongside this work. The final registry adoption follows the actual reviewed exercises; do not simply flip placeholder status. One identifiable per-target mapping and review result is enough to organise this job. Reuse applicable evidence and avoid launching another full-book rewrite or a new review bureaucracy.
