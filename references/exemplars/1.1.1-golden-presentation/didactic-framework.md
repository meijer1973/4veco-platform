# Didactic Framework

Status: conceptual route and review framework for the accepted §1.1.1 exemplar.

## Learning Claim

The presentation teaches students to understand schaarste, identify alternatieve kosten, and use the B02 four-step comparison route to reason about choices.

## Sequence Logic

1. Start with a route contract so students know what they are learning.
2. Anchor the idea in Lisa's budget tension before naming the concept.
3. Define schaarste as a relation between behoeften and middelen.
4. Transfer the relation to other actors so the concept does not stay tied to Lisa.
5. Define alternatieve kosten from the same choice case.
6. Prevent common misconceptions before procedure use.
7. Make the B02 procedure visible as a student action route.
8. Split the worked example into calculation/setup and interpretation/conclusion.
9. Add a small Lisa/farmer retrieval check to reveal immediate understanding.
10. End with a summary and bridge to the next paragraph.

## Misconception Control

The exemplar explicitly blocks:

- schaarste = zeldzaamheid;
- alternatieve kosten = prijs;
- alternatieve kosten = som van alle niet-gekozen opties;
- highest visible opbrengst = complete economic conclusion;
- skipping nettowaarde after identifying the best alternative.

## Worked Example Standard

A worked example should show:

- what is chosen;
- what is not chosen;
- which non-chosen alternative is best;
- what the alternative costs are;
- how nettowaarde is calculated;
- what the result means in words.

## Retrieval Check Boundary

The retrieval check is formative and short. It may test immediate understanding inside the presentation route, but it may not replace the paragraph's exercise set, short check, exit ticket, diagnostics, mastery evidence, or summative use.
