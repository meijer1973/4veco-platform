## Findings

- Learning goal alignment: PASS. The task sequence asks students to build a
  P-Q diagram from a table, read an interval before giving a numeric read-off,
  construct the linear formula, and calculate/check a percentage claim. That
  sequence is aligned with the paragraph's graph/table operation.
- Prior knowledge fit: PASS WITH FLAGS. The exit ticket assumes students have
  already practiced P-Q axes, table-point graph construction, interpolation,
  formulas, and percentage change. That is appropriate for an exit ticket, but
  it must not be used as first instruction.
- Didactic sequence: PASS. The route starts from source/table context, moves to
  graph construction, then reading, formula construction, and calculation. This
  preserves the economic reasoning order instead of offering a shortcut answer.
- Formative feedback: PASS. Feedback and route cards point to named practice
  surfaces without claiming a grade, diagnosis, mastery state, or summative
  result.
- Differentiation/transfer: PASS WITH FLAGS. The route cards are actionable and
  useful after a miss, but there is no adaptive sequencing or diagnostic
  decision. That boundary is correct and must remain explicit.
