# Adaptation notes — from §1.1.1 Keuzekompas to §1.1.2 Indexcheck

## What transferred successfully

- a prominent but non-answer-giving goal;
- source-left / task-right layout;
- three short tasks with a visible route;
- one-click normal actions;
- immediate selected state;
- meaningful distractors;
- individual repair;
- final exam-style answer assembly;
- feedback after checking;
- no internal tags.

## What was deliberately not transferred

- scarcity-evidence selection;
- “best alternative given up” cards;
- the §1.1.1 content model;
- a causal flowchart;
- a generic fragment bank for every task.

Those mechanics would not expose the central reasoning difficulty of §1.1.2.

## New reasoning model

```text
What does the numerical difference measure?
        ↓
Which old level is the correct reference?
        ↓
Do price data and index data produce the same percentage?
        ↓
How should the original claim be rewritten?
```

## Transfer test

The transfer succeeds when the new game feels like the same product family while demanding a different kind of economic thought.

It fails when:

- the old mechanics are copied despite poor fit;
- the game becomes a calculation worksheet;
- the interface gives the denominator away;
- “indexpunten” and “procenten” are treated as synonyms;
- the final explanation is hidden in a generic textarea.
