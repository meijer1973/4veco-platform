# Quality brief — §1.1.1 Choice Compass reasoning game

## Why the previous direction failed

The previous flowchart version was clunky because it asked students to manage a long ordered chain before the economic idea was clear. The challenge came from the UI, not from economics.

The new version makes the economic move simpler and more direct:

1. Find evidence of scarcity.
2. Identify the best forgone alternative.
3. Build a concise test answer.

## Why this is better

- It fits §1.1.1 instead of copying a price-mechanism pattern.
- It uses one-click selections.
- It avoids six-step chain overload.
- It avoids visible metadata tags.
- It includes plausible misconceptions as distractors.
- It preserves the key economics:
  - scarcity;
  - choice;
  - opportunity costs;
  - best alternative, not all alternatives together.

## What the team must preserve

The implementation must preserve the rendered experience, not only the data contract. A product that validates but renders as a long flowchart, a generic textarea, or fixed-order cards is not faithful to this exemplar.

## Review outcome target

A good reviewer should be able to say:

> This game is intuitively playable. The student is thinking about scarcity and opportunity costs, not about the control scheme.
