# Reasoning Flow Golden Exemplar v3 — quality brief

## Why v3 is better

v2 improved the surface, but the prominent goal gave away too much of the actual answer. It also asked students to distinguish `daardoor` and `waardoor`, which risks turning an economics-reasoning game into a Dutch connector trap.

v3 fixes this.

## Product quality bar demonstrated

A reasoning game should make economic reasoning visible without giving the answer away. The student builds the explanation through a small number of clear actions:

```text
read source → order cards → place connector words → build a test sentence
```

The interface now supports that standard:

- the goal is prominent but does not reveal the answer chain;
- the source remains compact;
- cards do not reveal their hidden role;
- mechanisms use concrete exam language such as `vraagoverschot` and `schaarste`;
- connector rows become proper sentences;
- `daardoor` can be reused for repeated cause-effect links;
- `waardoor` is not assessed as a separate connector in this economics game;
- the final answer builder uses clear slots, but the cards do not carry role labels;
- feedback comes after checking.

## Connector decision

You are right to simplify. Active production of both `daardoor` and `waardoor` is a language nuance. It may be worth teaching later as writing polish, but it is not the core skill in a paragraph reasoning game about an economic mechanism. If both are included, the evaluator must either accept reviewed equivalents or the sentence frame must make the grammatical target explicit.

For this exemplar, `daardoor` is the default cause-effect connector, `terwijl` marks the unchanged supply condition, and `dus` marks the conclusion.

## Standard to preserve

Do not weaken this back into:

- generic multiple choice;
- many unrelated game modes;
- visible metadata tags on cards;
- vague economic labels;
- a goal that gives away the full answer chain;
- a non-reusable connector bank where `daardoor` is needed twice;
- forced `waardoor` distinction without a language target;
- connector fragments that do not produce sentences;
- hidden always-correct controls;
- initial-load screenshots without interaction-state proof.
