## Purpose

This owned source defines the intended mature course shape: an 11-book,
three-year, exam-grounded curriculum contract. It preserves v5 as the detailed
Year 1 / Books 1-4 baseline and supplies the book-level Year 2 and Year 3
umbrella so later work can be reviewed against one coherent route.

This file does not mint paragraphs, MTUs, target exercises, exam-operation
records, product routes, or student-facing lessons. It is an owned umbrella
planning authority that reconciles:

- v4 as historical design evidence;
- v5 as the active four-book / four-test-week Year 1 source;
- `knowledge/three Year blue print.md` as rough later-year concept input;
- the product vision and product end-state;
- the reference roadmap's exam-ingestion north star.

## End-State Statement

The mature 4veco course blueprint is an 11-book, three-year exam-training spine. Every formal test week maps to one book, and every book maps to a cumulative path toward VWO economics exam capability.

Each exam-relevant concept, calculation, graph/table operation, source-reading operation, reasoning form, and answer-writing form must be introduced, practised, consolidated, retrieved, and finally tested in CvTE-style contexts.

## Core Decisions

- The final course uses 11 books, not the older 13-book concept.
- The formal assessment calendar is 4 + 4 + 3 books across three years.
- Each formal test week corresponds to one book.
- v6 is the three-year umbrella planning authority for book-level route,
  assessment-calendar structure, and sequencing.
- v5 remains the detailed Year 1 / Books 1-4 baseline until a later explicit
  migration replaces or absorbs those details.
- Books 1-10 do not regain separate printed test-preparation chapters by default; each book should carry cumulative retrieval and web-only test-preparation support unless a later reviewed book design explicitly authorizes another model.
- Book 11 carries final exam training and cumulative integration; it is not followed by a separate final exam book.
- Paragraph-level production waits for reviewed target exercises and
  exam-operation evidence.
- Machine references remain CLI-only.

## Year 2 Draft Scope

### Book 5 - Time, Stock-Flow, Pensions, Intertemporal Exchange

Book 5 builds the time layer that later finance and macro questions assume: stocks versus flows, intertemporal exchange, pension systems, indexation regimes, ageing, and sustainability reasoning.

Expected operation families:

- distinguish stock and flow variables;
- reason over time horizons and generations;
- calculate indexed, frozen, or wage-linked values when target evidence requires it;
- use source tables to evaluate pension and sustainability claims;
- write balanced policy explanations.

### Book 6 - Interest, Bonds, Housing, Financial Markets

Book 6 builds the finance bridge: nominal and real interest, bond price logic, household finance, housing, exchange-rate basics, saving balances, and financial-market applications.

Expected operation families:

- calculate and interpret interest-rate changes;
- explain bond-price and interest-rate direction;
- connect household choices to market conditions;
- read financial tables and source claims;
- prepare monetary-policy prerequisites.

### Book 7 - Risk, Insurance, Asymmetric Information

Book 7 teaches risk and information problems that make otherwise simple market models difficult: expected value, insurance pooling, adverse selection, moral hazard, principal-agent problems, transaction costs, and exchange-rate risk.

Expected operation families:

- compute expected value or expected loss where required;
- identify moral hazard and adverse selection in sources;
- explain how contracts, deductibles, premiums, and screening change incentives;
- build source-supported answers using cause-effect chains.

### Book 8 - Game Theory, Public Goods, Redistribution, Growth/Public Finance Bridge

Book 8 compresses the older bridge-book idea into the final Year 2 book. It joins strategic interaction, public goods, collective action, redistribution, debt, taxation, growth, productivity, circular economy, and green GDP at book level.

Internal spine:

| Segment | Focus | Load control |
|---|---|---|
| 8A | Strategic interaction: dominant strategy, Nash, prisoner's dilemma | Keep payoff logic and equilibrium reasoning together. |
| 8B | Collective action and public goods: free riding, intervention, welfare | Use public-good cases to retrieve welfare and intervention logic from Books 2-4. |
| 8C | Redistribution and public finance: tax, debt, redistribution, incentives | Tie fiscal choices to incentives and distribution; avoid full macro-policy drift. |
| 8D | Growth and sustainability bridge: productivity, green GDP, circular economy | Prepare Book 9 macro without turning Book 8 into a macro book. |

Expected operation families:

- identify dominant strategy and Nash-equilibrium patterns;
- explain prisoner-dilemma and collective-good outcomes;
- evaluate public intervention and redistribution trade-offs;
- connect growth, sustainability, debt, and public finance to policy choices.

## Year 3 Draft Scope

### Book 9 - Keynesian Macro And Fiscal Policy

Book 9 starts the macro-policy year: Keynesian demand, multiplier, output gap, automatic stabilisers, anticyclical fiscal policy, shocks, inflation channels, and labour-market channels.

Expected operation families:

- calculate or interpret multiplier effects where target evidence requires it;
- identify output gaps and demand/supply shocks;
- choose and justify fiscal measures;
- read macro graphs, tables, and policy-source excerpts.

### Book 10 - Monetary Policy, IS-MB-GA, Open Economy

Book 10 covers monetary transmission, central-bank decisions, IS-MB-GA or related policy representations, exchange-rate channels, open-economy constraints, and policy trade-offs. More technical constructs such as trilemma reasoning or interest parity require explicit exam or target-exercise anchors before paragraph production.

Expected operation families:

- trace monetary policy transmission;
- interpret IS-MB-GA or related macro-policy representations;
- explain exchange-rate effects on trade, inflation, and competitiveness;
- compare policy mixes under constraints.

### Book 11 - Integrated Policy And Final Exam Training

Book 11 is the cumulative exam book. It folds the older "advanced integration" and "final exam training" books into one test-week scope.

Internal spine:

| Segment | Focus | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 11A | Exam operation decomposition: prompt, source, graph/table, calculation, reasoning, answer form | Students learn to unpack mixed CvTE-style prompts before solving. |
| 11B | Mixed domain retrieval: micro, finance, risk, macro in one case | Students retrieve across Books 1-10 under realistic source pressure. |
| 11C | Correction-model discipline: point allocation, command words, answer construction | Students practise scoring logic and answer-form precision. |
| 11D | Timed full-paper practice: official-style papers, review, repair, retest | Students build time discipline through feedback and retest loops. |

Expected operation families:

- decompose mixed CvTE-style prompts into concepts, sources, calculations, graphs/tables, reasoning, and answer forms;
- practise full correction-model answer construction;
- retrieve micro, institution, finance, risk, information, and macro concepts in mixed cases;
- manage time and scoring discipline across full papers.

## Compression Decision: 13 Books To 11 Books

Decision: the final course uses 11 books, not 13.

Reason: the intended formal assessment structure is 4 + 4 + 3 test weeks.

Consequences:

- The older Book 9 growth/public-finance bridge is folded into Book 8 and, where macro-policy evidence requires it, Book 9.
- The older Book 12 advanced integration and Book 13 final exam training are folded into Book 11.
- Final exam training becomes the dominant function of Book 11 and a cumulative layer running through Year 3, not a separate twelfth or thirteenth book.
