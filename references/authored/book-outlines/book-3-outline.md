## Shared authoring contract


Use backward design: proposed goals -> reviewed target -> observable operations -> explanation, worked example and practice. Every required operation must be explicitly taught or demonstrably retrieved; a topic encountered only as a preview is not secure prior learning.

Preserve the inherited Book 2+ printed exercise sequence: **Uitgewerkt voorbeeld; Startopgaven; Begeleide inoefening; Zelfstandige oefening; Doeloefening; Denkertje / Bonusopgave; Herhaling / Herhaling en interleaving**. Guided practice is a printed support option, not an extra destination. The core is Startopgaven -> Zelfstandige oefening -> Doeloefening. A teacher may assign the prerequisite part of Startopgaven at the beginning of the lesson without rearranging the printed headings. Mixed-exercise paragraphs are consolidation lessons: do not force a new-theory template onto them.

Plan a complete 55-minute lesson, including instruction and the worked example, not just student exercise time. Use 3-5 minutes of relevant prerequisite retrieval within the compact start work, then make the previous technique necessary again in the current worked example, independent practice and target. Closing review supplies spaced practice in other already-taught operations. Do not turn all seven printed sections into a compulsory one-lesson workload.

First transfer to an unfamiliar context requires a short explicit bridge: identify the actors, rename the variables, preserve the calculation steps, explain the changed interpretation, then fade the support. New economic meaning must not be disguised as mere revision.

All essential material remains paper-first and independent of devices. Use Dutch suitable for 4 VWO, established economic terminology and short source-specific conclusions. Use 'productiecapaciteit', not newly coined student-facing substitutes. A blank table must have an explicit completion instruction before questions refer to its results. Give every graph axis a variable, unit and usable scale; distinguish a market graph from a firm graph. Plan visuals to support actual target operations, not as decoration.

Inherited working print assumption for later production: carry forward the Book 2 ceiling of 40 pages per chapter for explanation plus exercises, excluding answers. This proposal is not a rendered-page feasibility certificate. The longer chapters require early page and timing trials; do not recover space through unreadably small type or by deleting necessary practice. Test preparation is not a separate printed chapter.

## Book-level decision

**Proposed title: Boek 3 — Overheidsingrijpen, concurrentie en internationale handel.**

Retain **14 count-bearing paragraphs: 6 + 4 + 4**. Chapters 3.1 and 3.2 stay in place. Move the previous proposal's four trade paragraphs from 4.3 to 3.3. Move its four monopoly paragraphs from 3.3 to the opening of Book 4, now 4.1.

The learning rhythm is **intervention techniques -> mathematical firm decisions -> conceptual and source-based trade reasoning**. The final chapter remains economically demanding but does not become a second course in optimisation or a sequence of long tariff-area calculations.

At book exit, students should be able to analyse familiar market interventions, connect a competitive market to the firm's production decision, and explain specialisation, trade and protection using sources, graphs and a small amount of already-taught arithmetic. Monopoly, price discrimination and formal labour-market analysis are not Book 3 exit requirements.

## Dependency and balance decisions

Trade needs Chapters 1.3, 2.3 and 3.1, not monopoly. It can therefore follow competitive-firm analysis without assuming anything from Book 4. Preserve four safeguards:

1. Comparative advantage remains qualitative. No production-table specialisation routine, calculated opportunity-cost ratios or exchange-ratio bounds return through examples, targets or compulsory practice.
2. Labour productivity and unit labour costs are not prior knowledge in trade. Keep competitiveness explanations qualitative with explicit source information. Formal labour calculations are taught later in §4.3.1 and can then revisit the trade context.
3. Use a supplied, readable graph or short source table as the default trade representation. Full unaided graph construction and several simultaneous welfare-area calculations are not mandatory in every target.
4. Plan at most one short chain of familiar numerical operations per trade theory target, accompanied by substantive explanation and source questions. This is a proposed load guardrail, not measured timing evidence. Retain real calculation practice without replacing the conceptual chapter by a long worksheet.

## Chapter 3.1 — Overheidsingrijpen

**Organising question:** How do interventions change prices, transactions, distribution and welfare?

**Purpose:** Students reuse the Book 1 market model and Book 2 elasticity and surplus techniques to analyse one intervention at a time. The new learning is the intervention mechanism, not a replacement calculation system.

**Entry prerequisites:** Book 1: demand, supply, equations, equilibrium and shifts. Book 2: percentage changes, demand responsiveness, consumer/producer surplus and welfare loss. Retrieve these selectively before their first use.

### 3.1.1 Belastingen: wig en nieuw evenwicht

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Distinguish the price paid by buyers from the amount received by sellers; represent a per-unit tax and calculate the new traded quantity.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve linear equilibrium and substitution from §§1.3.2-1.3.3. The two-price interpretation and tax wedge are new formal learning.

**Proposed target brief:** From a short market case, identify the original equilibrium, introduce the tax, calculate buyer price, seller receipt and transactions, and interpret the wedge.

**Visual teaching plan:** One before/after market diagram with both prices attached to the same traded quantity; a matching calculation sequence.

**Boundary and review focus:** State who remits the tax, its unit, and the competitive model assumptions. Remittance is not the same as economic tax burden. Do not add welfare accounting before §3.1.2.

### 3.1.2 Belastingdruk en welvaartsverlies

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Calculate the division of the tax burden, tax revenue and welfare consequences; explain the effect of relative responsiveness.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve §3.1.1, Book 2 elasticity interpretation and surplus-area calculations. Qualitative supply responsiveness does not require introducing a new supply-elasticity formula.

**Proposed target brief:** Use one tax case to allocate the burden, calculate government revenue and relevant surplus changes, and explain one distributional claim from the evidence.

**Visual teaching plan:** A tax rectangle and lost-surplus triangle, plus one carefully controlled responsiveness contrast.

**Boundary and review focus:** Government revenue is a transfer, not the deadweight loss. The initial welfare benchmark excludes external effects; corrective taxation returns in §4.2.4. Do not infer elasticity merely from slopes plotted with different scales.

### 3.1.3 Subsidies

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Represent a per-unit subsidy, calculate both prices and transactions, and identify government expenditure and distributional effects.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse the tax-wedge method with the direction reversed, and retrieve surplus calculations. Explicitly compare what changes and what stays the same.

**Proposed target brief:** Calculate a subsidy outcome and government expenditure, identify beneficiaries, and qualify a claim that a subsidy must increase welfare.

**Visual teaching plan:** Paired tax/subsidy diagrams with consistent notation and a separate government-budget rectangle.

**Boundary and review focus:** Specify the eligible transaction and payment recipient. In this first treatment assume no external benefit; §4.2.5 deliberately changes that assumption. A subsidy is not automatically an efficiency improvement.

### 3.1.4 Maximumprijs

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Distinguish binding and non-binding price ceilings; calculate demand, supply, actual transactions and shortage.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve equilibrium and quantity-at-price calculations. Reuse surplus areas only after the transaction and allocation rules have been specified.

**Proposed target brief:** Analyse a regulated-price case, determine whether the ceiling binds, calculate the shortage and actual sales, and explain who may fail to obtain the product.

**Visual teaching plan:** A ceiling diagram with Qv, Qa and actual transactions separately labelled; one non-binding contrast.

**Boundary and review focus:** A low quoted price does not mean everyone buys at that price. Welfare-area calculations require an explicit allocation assumption. Avoid turning this into a full housing-policy chapter.

### 3.1.5 Minimumprijs en quota

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Analyse a binding price floor and distinguish it from a production quota. Calculate surplus supply and, when specified, public purchases.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Transfer the ceiling procedure; reuse government-expenditure rectangles and the distinction between offered and actually traded quantities.

**Proposed target brief:** Use one supported-price case with an explicit purchase rule, then a bounded quota comparison. Calculate the quantities and budget consequences actually asked for.

**Visual teaching plan:** Separate diagrams for a floor and a quota; do not stack several new interventions into the first example.

**Boundary and review focus:** A production quota is not automatically a price floor; a floor does not imply public purchases unless the source says so. This is a high-load paragraph: the quota comparison stays bounded. Prepares the import-quota comparison in §3.3.3 and the later minimum-wage application in §4.3.4.

### 3.1.6 Gemengde opgaven: overheidsingrijpen

**Role:** Consolidation; no new theory.

**Intended learning:** Select the appropriate intervention model and integrate price, quantity, budget and welfare reasoning without new theory.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Interleave §§3.1.1-3.1.5 with Book 2 elasticity and surplus. Require method selection rather than identifying the method in every question heading.

**Proposed target brief:** Use a small set of contrasting policy cases. At least one target combines a numerical outcome, a labelled diagram and a source-based policy conclusion.

**Visual teaching plan:** Consistent before/after diagrams and short source tables, with support faded to the required target level.

**Boundary and review focus:** No new intervention or externality model. Do not force every intervention into one unwieldy target or one 55-minute compulsory route.

## Chapter 3.2 — Volkomen concurrentie

**Organising question:** How can an individual firm choose output when the market determines its selling price?

**Purpose:** Connect market equilibrium to firm-level price taking, profit-maximising output, and entry or exit in the long run.

**Entry prerequisites:** Book 2: total and average costs, revenue, profit, break-even and marginal quantities. Book 1: equilibrium and supply shifts. Formal output optimisation is new here, not presumed from Book 2.

### 3.2.1 Volkomen concurrentie: kenmerken

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Explain price taking and connect the market price to the individual firm’s GO and MO.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve market equilibrium and TO = P x Q; re-use the distinction between total, average and marginal revenue.

**Proposed target brief:** Read a market graph, transfer the price to a firm graph and explain why the firm cannot profitably choose any price it likes.

**Visual teaching plan:** Adjacent market and firm graphs with visibly different quantity scales and clearly linked prices.

**Boundary and review focus:** Explain the stated competitive assumptions. A horizontal firm demand curve is not the same as a horizontal market demand curve.

### 3.2.2 Winstmaximalisatie bij volkomen concurrentie

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Use marginal comparison to select output and calculate total profit, linking MO, MK, price and average total cost.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve Book 2 cost/revenue calculations and marginal meaning. Explicitly teach the optimisation rule; do not label it revision.

**Proposed target brief:** Obtain the relevant marginal relation, determine the profit-maximising feasible output, calculate profit and interpret the result in a firm diagram.

**Visual teaching plan:** An incremental decision table followed by a firm diagram with the selected quantity and a profit rectangle.

**Boundary and review focus:** If a quadratic total-cost function is used, teach the limited quadratic-to-linear derivative operation within this paragraph rather than assuming prior calculus. Check the direction of marginal comparisons and production capacity; MO = MK alone is not a universal maximum proof. Flag the combined mathematical and economic load for target/timing review.

### 3.2.3 Langetermijnevenwicht

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Explain how entry or exit changes market supply, price and firm profit, and interpret the competitive long-run outcome.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve §3.2.2, market supply shifts, GTK and break-even. Re-use calculations to support the adjustment narrative.

**Proposed target brief:** Start from profit or loss, explain entry or exit, draw the direction of adjustment and identify the resulting firm outcome under the stated assumptions.

**Visual teaching plan:** A short market-to-firm adjustment sequence rather than a dense all-at-once graph.

**Boundary and review focus:** The familiar zero-economic-profit result requires the specified competitive entry/exit and cost assumptions. It does not mean entrepreneurs receive no remuneration or that total revenue is zero.

### 3.2.4 Gemengde opgaven: volkomen concurrentie

**Role:** Consolidation; no new theory.

**Intended learning:** Integrate market price formation, firm output, profit and long-run adjustment.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Interleave Book 2 total/average/marginal distinctions, equilibrium, and §§3.2.1-3.2.3.

**Proposed target brief:** A market change leads to a new firm calculation and an explanation of subsequent entry or exit; students connect market and firm evidence.

**Visual teaching plan:** Paired diagrams and a cost table or function, using consistent units.

**Boundary and review focus:** No monopoly or new long-run cost theory. Monopoly is taught next book, in Chapter 4.1; it is not a prerequisite for the trade chapter that follows here. A multi-stage exercise still requires an explicit initial situation and adjustment horizon.

## Chapter 3.3 — Internationale handel

**Organising question:** Why do countries trade, who is affected, and how should we assess protection?

**Purpose:** End Book 3 with a more conceptual application of familiar market tools. The chapter has three theory/application paragraphs plus one mixed-exercise paragraph. Retain specialisation, comparative advantage, the world-price model and protection from the previous proposal, but reduce compulsory calculation density. Explanations must connect a mechanism, a source or diagram, and a qualified conclusion.

**Entry prerequisites:** Book 1 scarcity/opportunity-cost meaning, demand/supply, equilibrium and source reading; Book 2 consumer/producer surplus and familiar calculations; Chapter 3.1 tax, subsidy and quota mechanisms. Competitive-market assumptions can be retrieved from §3.2.1. Monopoly, price discrimination, formal productivity/unit-labour-cost calculations, labour equilibrium and later macroeconomic models are not prerequisites.

**Mathematical scope:** Keep quantity-at-price, import/export gaps and one simple tariff-revenue calculation as transfer of taught techniques. Do not introduce a new comparative-advantage algorithm. Do not require full balance-of-payments accounting, exchange-rate determination, differentiated firm functions or formal strategic games.

### 3.3.1 Waarom landen handelen: specialisatie en concurrentiepositie

**Role:** Conceptual theory and source-based application.

**Intended learning:** Explain specialisation, absolute versus comparative advantage and possible mutual gains qualitatively. Distinguish an explanation of relative opportunity cost from a general claim about a country's production strengths or competitiveness.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve scarcity, choice and the meaning of opportunity cost from Book 1. Introduce import/export and the new comparative-advantage meaning explicitly. Keep discussion of productivity, quality or costs qualitative and supported by information in the source; no formal labour-cost formula is assumed.

**Proposed target brief:** Use a short qualitative two-country case, including one in which the same country is more productive in both activities. Explain why relative advantage matters and how exchange may benefit both. Assess a short claim that cheaper production or greater productivity necessarily makes every participant better off. Require a reasoned answer, not a numerical specialisation table.

**Visual teaching plan:** A simple goods-flow sketch and an annotated qualitative relative-strength comparison. No production-capacity table that implicitly asks students to calculate a specialisation solution.

**Balance choice:** The target is explanation-led, with no required numerical comparative-advantage calculation. A compact source supplies the facts needed for the reasoning; students need not import labour-market knowledge from Book 4.

**Boundary and review focus:** No calculated opportunity-cost ratios, production-possibility-frontier optimisation, exchange-ratio bounds or separate ruilvoet calculation objective. Do not equate absolute with comparative advantage or claim that all groups necessarily gain. The conceptual/no-calculation boundary follows the owner's retained design instruction; this revision makes no new CE/SE validation claim. Formal productivity and unit-labour-cost analysis is deferred to §4.3.1.

### 3.3.2 Wereldmarktprijs, import, export en welvaart

**Role:** Conceptual market interpretation with bounded numerical transfer.

**Intended learning:** Explain how a stated world price changes domestic production and consumption; identify whether the country imports or exports; calculate the trade gap and interpret the effects on domestic buyers and sellers.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse reading a demand/supply graph, determining quantity at a stated price, and the meaning of consumer/producer surplus. The new distinction is between domestic production, domestic consumption and the foreign trade flow, not a new equation-solving system.

**Proposed target brief:** Provide a labelled domestic market graph or a short table and a world price. Read or calculate domestic supply and demand, determine the import/export gap, and explain a buyer and seller outcome with evidence. Ask one qualitative surplus/distribution question. Include the reversed-price/export case in accessible practice without a second long calculation chain.

**Visual teaching plan:** A world-price line through familiar domestic demand and supply. Mark domestic production, domestic consumption and the gap separately. Use simple shading to support surplus interpretation rather than automatically requiring all areas to be calculated.

**Balance choice:** One short numerical chain supports the explanation. If algebra is selected, use familiar linear relations and easy values; do not simultaneously require full graph construction and multiple numerical welfare comparisons.

**Boundary and review focus:** State a small, price-taking country, competitive markets, sufficient foreign supply/demand and the relevant transport/tariff assumptions. Keep currencies fixed. Domestic demand is not domestic production. A rise in the stated total-surplus measure is not evidence that every group gains. The target need not produce a full welfare-area ledger or balance-of-payments account.

### 3.3.3 Protectionisme: invoerheffingen en importquota

**Role:** Policy explanation with one bounded tariff application.

**Intended learning:** Explain why a government might restrict imports and distinguish its stated aim from effects on different groups. Use the familiar market model to interpret a per-unit import tariff; calculate remaining imports and tariff revenue in a simple case. Contrast an import quota qualitatively.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse §3.3.2's world-price model and Chapter 3.1's tax, government-budget and quota concepts. Explicitly distinguish a tariff on imported units from a tax on all domestic sales.

**Proposed target brief:** Present one short policy source and a supplied before/after graph or quantity table. Determine the remaining imported quantity and tariff receipts, then explain effects on domestic consumers and producers. Evaluate one bounded argument for the policy. Use a short non-calculation contrast to explain why a quota need not create the same government revenue.

**Visual teaching plan:** Before/after price lines on the same domestic market graph; mark the import gap and a single tariff-revenue rectangle. A small stakeholder comparison supports reasoning without substituting labels for explanation.

**Balance choice:** Keep the numerical target to one familiar chain. Do not routinely require a new-equilibrium derivation, four surplus calculations, two welfare-loss triangles and a quota-allocation calculation in the same lesson. Quantitative welfare repetition can be selected later where it is already taught and fits the target, but is not the default gate for this paragraph.

**Boundary and review focus:** Use imported quantity, not all domestic sales, for tariff revenue. Teach the no-import boundary using graph reading or a supplied contrast; do not mechanically impose world price plus tariff after imports disappear. State the model assumptions behind the case. Quantitative quota rents/allocation, export-subsidy models, exchange-rate adjustment and formal strategic retaliation remain deferred. An industry-gain claim is not automatically an economy-wide welfare claim.

### 3.3.4 Gemengde opgaven: internationale handel

**Role:** Consolidation; no new theory.

**Intended learning:** Select and combine qualitative trade explanation, familiar world-price interpretation, a short import/tariff calculation and a source-supported conclusion. Distinguish aggregate and distributional claims.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve §§3.3.1-3.3.3 and already-taught market, budget and surplus meanings. No monopoly or labour-market procedure is needed. A source may mention jobs in ordinary language, but must not require labour-demand equations, participation statistics or unit-labour-cost calculations.

**Proposed target brief:** Use one compact trade-policy case plus a short contrasting claim. Students select the relevant evidence, complete one manageable numerical chain from familiar graph/table data, explain effects on different groups and give a qualified conclusion. Comparative-advantage reasoning stays verbal. Supply or directly teach any contextual information not already in the course.

**Visual teaching plan:** A familiar domestic market graph and a short source or stakeholder table; no new diagram family is introduced in consolidation.

**Balance choice:** This is a reasoning-led capstone, not a disguised return to a long calculation-heavy exam paper. Keep the support faded to independent source interpretation while maintaining manageable reading load and explicit question wording.

**Boundary and review focus:** The earlier proposal cites a 2022 VWO-I Importheffing task as a possible target-design input. Carry that reference forward for later source verification, not as a completed adaptation or evidence that every operation is approved. Later current-account and game-theory demands stay outside this chapter. Claims about protected jobs do not require or establish a formal economy-wide employment result.
