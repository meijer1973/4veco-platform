## Book-level decision

Retain **14 paragraphs in 6 + 4 + 4**. Chapter 3.1 stays intact. Chapter 3.2 now teaches price taking, then the limited derivative, then short-run output/profit choice, followed by independent consolidation. Its former long-run paragraph moves to the first lesson of Book 4. Chapter 3.3 remains the complete four-lesson trade chapter.

The economic rhythm remains **intervention → firm decisions → conceptual/source-led trade**. The new boundary is explicit: Book 3 completes the price-taking firm's decision in a given short-run setting; Book 4 first asks whether that profit persists when the number of firms can adjust. Free entry may be named as a competitive feature in Book 3, but its formal adjustment and zero-economic-profit outcome are not Book 3 assessment requirements.

## Working design rules

One numbered paragraph is one complete **55-minute core lesson**, not a heading that routinely spans two periods. This is a design requirement; the new outline is not a classroom timing certificate. The Year 1 allocation stays **12 + 12 + 14 + 17 = 55**. The selected split is funded by a real deferral, not by deleting consolidation or moving a second new-learning phase into homework.

Keep the current printed sequence: **Uitgewerkt voorbeeld; Startopgaven; Begeleide inoefening; Zelfstandige oefening; Doeloefening; Denkertje / Bonusopgave; Herhaling / Herhaling en interleaving**. The core is Startopgaven → Zelfstandige oefening → Doeloefening; guided practice is an alternative support route to the same destination, not a second compulsory set. Mixed paragraphs remain consolidation lessons without new theory. Preserve paper-first teaching, appropriate Dutch for 4 vwo, explicit units and capacity assumptions, source-supported conclusions and genuine visual support.

Use the existing delivered text, examples, targets and answers as the starting material. Change what the approved lesson boundary requires; do not rebuild valid paragraphs merely because an old registry contains a placeholder. For every changed target, map observable operations to teaching, practice and an answer model. Review the target's actual mathematics, economic assumptions and assessment coverage before final adoption; the book's existence alone is not approval.

Estimate the selected questions and their reading, drawing, calculation and explanation steps alongside instruction, example, transitions and feedback **before** rewriting a complete paragraph. Teacher-guide prose can follow the book revision, but lesson feasibility cannot wait until then. Support and bonus may stay printed; required new learning or independent evidence cannot be relabelled optional solely to obtain 55 minutes.

The other overloads identified in F08 remain open. No additional split, merger or goal deferral is authorised by this v3 scope. Retained high-risk targets require a credible selected core; where that cannot be achieved without changing learning goals, return the precise remaining trade-off to the owner. Do not silently restore routine two-lesson paragraphs.

The outline specifies the intended scope and sequence. The delivered manuscripts and PDFs specify the existing content to preserve. During this bounded revision, older blueprint placement and placeholder target records are **migration inputs**, not instructions to reverse the selected design. A brief transition note must name the revision and scope before implementation. External requirements, mathematical correctness, protected-source rules and publication/merge permissions are not overridden. ## Chapter 3.1 — Overheidsingrijpen

**Organising question:** How do interventions change prices, transactions, distribution and welfare?

Use the Book 1 market model and Book 2 elasticity/surplus to analyse one intervention at a time. Retain taxes, subsidies, ceilings, floors, the bounded production-quota comparison and the mixed lesson. In particular, do not move minimum prices merely to balance books: doing so would weaken the complete intervention toolkit and its trade/labour dependencies.

**Entry and exit:** demand/supply equations, percentage changes and surplus meanings are incoming; two-price wedges and policy outcomes are taught here. Externality accounting remains in Chapter 4.2.

**Timing status:** F08 remains open for §§3.1.2, 3.1.3 and 3.1.5. Their content and goals are retained, not certified to fit 55 minutes by this outline. Inspect the actual selected core and duplicated practice before proposing any further budget-neutral scope decision.

### 3.1.1 Belastingen: wig en nieuw evenwicht

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Distinguish the price paid by buyers from the amount received by sellers; represent a per-unit tax and calculate the new traded quantity.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve linear equilibrium and substitution from §§1.3.2-1.3.3. The two-price interpretation and tax wedge are new formal learning.

**Target design brief:** From a short market case, identify the original equilibrium, introduce the tax, calculate buyer price, seller receipt and transactions, and interpret the wedge.

**Visual teaching plan:** One before/after market diagram with both prices attached to the same traded quantity; a matching calculation sequence.

**Boundary and review focus:** State who remits the tax, its unit, and the competitive model assumptions. Remittance is not the same as economic tax burden. Do not add welfare accounting before §3.1.2.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the delivered teaching and target by default; update only affected references and any separately justified repair.

### 3.1.2 Belastingdruk en welvaartsverlies

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Calculate the division of the tax burden, tax revenue and welfare consequences; explain the effect of relative responsiveness.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve §3.1.1, Book 2 elasticity interpretation and surplus-area calculations. Qualitative supply responsiveness does not require introducing a new supply-elasticity formula.

**Target design brief:** Use one tax case to allocate the burden, calculate government revenue and relevant surplus changes, and explain one distributional claim from the evidence.

**Visual teaching plan:** A tax rectangle and lost-surplus triangle, plus one carefully controlled responsiveness contrast.

**Boundary and review focus:** Government revenue is a transfer, not the deadweight loss. The initial welfare benchmark excludes external effects; corrective taxation returns in §4.2.4. Do not infer elasticity merely from slopes plotted with different scales.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the delivered teaching and target by default; update only affected references and any separately justified repair.

### 3.1.3 Subsidies

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Represent a per-unit subsidy, calculate both prices and transactions, and identify government expenditure and distributional effects.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse the tax-wedge method with the direction reversed, and retrieve surplus calculations. Explicitly compare what changes and what stays the same.

**Target design brief:** Calculate a subsidy outcome and government expenditure, identify beneficiaries, and qualify a claim that a subsidy must increase welfare.

**Visual teaching plan:** Paired tax/subsidy diagrams with consistent notation and a separate government-budget rectangle.

**Boundary and review focus:** Specify the eligible transaction and payment recipient. In this first treatment assume no external benefit; §4.2.5 deliberately changes that assumption. A subsidy is not automatically an efficiency improvement.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the delivered teaching and target by default; update only affected references and any separately justified repair.

### 3.1.4 Maximumprijs

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Distinguish binding and non-binding price ceilings; calculate demand, supply, actual transactions and shortage.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve equilibrium and quantity-at-price calculations. Reuse surplus areas only after the transaction and allocation rules have been specified.

**Target design brief:** Analyse a regulated-price case, determine whether the ceiling binds, calculate the shortage and actual sales, and explain who may fail to obtain the product.

**Visual teaching plan:** A ceiling diagram with Qv, Qa and actual transactions separately labelled; one non-binding contrast.

**Boundary and review focus:** A low quoted price does not mean everyone buys at that price. Welfare-area calculations require an explicit allocation assumption. Avoid turning this into a full housing-policy chapter.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the delivered teaching and target by default; update only affected references and any separately justified repair.

### 3.1.5 Minimumprijs en quota

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Analyse a binding price floor and distinguish it from a production quota. Calculate surplus supply and, when specified, public purchases.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Transfer the ceiling procedure; reuse government-expenditure rectangles and the distinction between offered and actually traded quantities.

**Target design brief:** Use one supported-price case with an explicit purchase rule, then a bounded quota comparison. Calculate the quantities and budget consequences actually asked for.

**Visual teaching plan:** Separate diagrams for a floor and a quota; do not stack several new interventions into the first example.

**Boundary and review focus:** A production quota is not automatically a price floor; a floor does not imply public purchases unless the source says so. This is a high-load paragraph: the quota comparison stays bounded. Prepares the import-quota comparison in §3.3.3 and the later minimum-wage application in §4.3.4.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the delivered teaching and target by default; update only affected references and any separately justified repair.

### 3.1.6 Gemengde opgaven: overheidsingrijpen

**Role:** Consolidation; no new theory.

**Intended learning:** Select the appropriate intervention model and integrate price, quantity, budget and welfare reasoning without new theory.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Interleave §§3.1.1-3.1.5 with Book 2 elasticity and surplus. Require method selection rather than identifying the method in every question heading.

**Target design brief:** Use a small set of contrasting policy cases. At least one target combines a numerical outcome, a labelled diagram and a source-based policy conclusion.

**Visual teaching plan:** Consistent before/after diagrams and short source tables, with support faded to the required target level.

**Boundary and review focus:** No new intervention or externality model. Do not force every intervention into one unwieldy target or one 55-minute compulsory route.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the delivered teaching and target by default; update only affected references and any separately justified repair.

## Chapter 3.2 — Volkomen concurrentie: productie en winst

**Organising question:** How does a price-taking firm choose output at a given price and production capacity?

Teach two distinct destinations in two numbered lessons: the limited derivative in §3.2.2 and economic output optimisation in §3.2.3. The former long-run paragraph is not pushed into either of them. Preserve one mixed lesson before the Book 3 assessment.

**Entry prerequisites:** Book 2 total/average/marginal costs and revenue, profit and break-even; Book 1 market equilibrium and shifts. Do not claim Book 2 already taught differentiation or formal optimisation.

**Exit boundary:** students can calculate and justify a short-run firm decision. The normal-remuneration interpretation, entry/exit mechanism and long-run zero-economic-profit endpoint are taught next book in §4.1.1. Keep the general definition of perfect competition intact while explicitly delimiting which adjustment is analysed now.

### 3.2.1 Volkomen concurrentie: kenmerken

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Explain price taking and connect the market price to the individual firm’s GO and MO.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve market equilibrium and TO = P x Q; re-use the distinction between total, average and marginal revenue.

**Target design brief:** Read a market graph, transfer the price to a firm graph and explain why the firm cannot profitably choose any price it likes.

**Visual teaching plan:** Adjacent market and firm graphs with visibly different quantity scales and clearly linked prices.

**Boundary and review focus:** Explain the stated competitive assumptions. A horizontal firm demand curve is not the same as a horizontal market demand curve.

**v3 implementation:** Retain price taking and the market-to-firm bridge. Free entry may remain a market characteristic; do not assess the entry/exit adjustment or the zero-economic-profit endpoint in Book 3.

### 3.2.2 Marginale kosten en de afgeleide

**Role:** Theory and application; the first half of the former §3.2.2.

**Intended learning:** Derive MK from TK = aq² + bq + c using only the quadratic, linear and constant terms required in the course. Evaluate and interpret the resulting marginal cost at a stated q with the correct units. Distinguish a point marginal value from the average additional cost over a finite table interval. Explain why fixed costs disappear from MK but remain in TK and profit.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve §2.1.3's ΔTK/Δq, substitution and total/per-unit distinctions. The derivative operation is new instruction here. Retrieve the revenue meaning of P = GO = MO from §3.2.1, but do not teach the optimisation rule as a second destination in this lesson.

**Target design brief:** Reuse an existing simple cost model from the delivered §3.2.2. Require the student to derive MK without a term-by-term scaffold, calculate its value at one stated output, compare it with a supplied or compactly calculated interval average, and explain the effect of a change in the fixed-cost term. Both the interval and point being compared must be explicit. Prepare a complete matching answer, not only a new title for old target 18.

**Visual teaching plan:** Link a short total-cost table to a labelled term-by-term derivative strip and a marginal-cost line with one evaluated point. The representation should explain what changes and what its units are; it is not a formal tangent/limit proof. Fade the term prompts before independent target work.

**Boundary and review focus:** No full calculus course, differentiation of GTK, general product/quotient rules, or price/output optimisation. Do not call ΔTK/Δq over a large interval the exact derivative at its endpoint. Preserve the existing fixed-cost/capacity convention. A full optimum, capacity comparison and profit rectangle belong to §3.2.3, not this target. This new paragraph must have its own worked preparation, independent practice and compact target.

**v3 implementation:** Split the actual manuscript around its mathematical learning destination. Reuse its derivative and interval/point explanations and suitable existing practice; do not duplicate the whole former exercise set.

### 3.2.3 Winstmaximalisatie bij volkomen concurrentie

**Role:** Theory and application; the second half of the former §3.2.2.

**Intended learning:** Use marginal comparisons to select a feasible output at a given market price. Obtain and check the MO = MK candidate, explain the directions on either side, check production capacity and relevant boundaries, calculate TO − TK, and represent profit through P and GTK at the same chosen q.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve the limited derivative from §3.2.2, price taking from §3.2.1, and the Book 2 total/average/marginal distinction. The economic output-choice rule is explicitly new. A short derivative retrieval item is appropriate; another full introductory derivative lesson is not.

**Target design brief:** Reuse the delivered 'Korrels voor kwekerijen' target model and its valid answers as the starting point. Ask for the marginal relation, feasible output, a justified maximum, total profit and the corresponding profit rectangle. Retain one bounded capacity contrast and the relevant q = 0 interpretation under explicit unavoidable-cost assumptions. Select the actual question chain for a complete one-lesson route; reuse rather than automatically append additional full optimisation cases.

**Visual teaching plan:** A marginal comparison leading to the firm diagram: given P → MO; known TK → MK; candidate q → feasibility → profit. Show why a rectangle with per-unit values differs from the vertical profit gap in a total-cost/total-revenue graph.

**Boundary and review focus:** MO = MK is not by itself a universal maximum proof. Do not choose output by mechanically minimising GTK. Fixed costs remain fixed within the stated period and capacity; avoid new production-range complications. Entry/exit, normal remuneration and the long-run zero-economic-profit result are formally taught in new §4.1.1, not here. Distinguish a short-period loss from an automatic decision to leave the market.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the economic decision chain from old §3.2.2 and treat its derivative component as retrieval from the immediately preceding lesson. This is not the former §3.2.3 long-run paragraph, despite reusing that numeric ID.

### 3.2.4 Gemengde opgaven: de prijsnemende onderneming

**Role:** Consolidation; no new theory.

**Intended learning:** Independently connect market price formation and a short-run change in that price to the output, costs and profit of one price-taking firm. Keep market Q, firm q, per-unit values and total amounts distinct. Select and check a feasible marginal output decision.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve §§3.2.1–3.2.3, Book 1 market equations and demand shifts, and Book 2 costs, revenue, averages and profit. The number of active firms and their production capacities are held fixed over the analysed adjustment window. The long-run mechanism is not an assessed prerequisite.

**Target design brief:** Adapt the existing Kweekkorrels case from three moments to two: the initial market and the immediate outcome after a demand change, before the number of firms adjusts. Keep the existing market equations, firm cost model, Q/q distinction, derivative, capacity check and profit representation where they remain consistent. Remove source C and the formal long-run operations in 36e–f from this Book 3 target. Replace only what is needed for a concise short-run interpretation or model check; do not expand the remaining arithmetic to fill the former target's length.

**Visual teaching plan:** Paired market/firm diagrams at the two stated moments. The market shift, changed firm price line and relevant profit rectangle are connected explicitly. No late-entry curve or minimum-GTK long-run endpoint is required.

**Boundary and review focus:** In the complete printed set, not just the target, relocate old exercise 35 and the long-run content of bonus 37 or replace them with already-taught retrieval. Reword old 36a's economic-profit/normal-remuneration premise as a supplied cost convention; do not require interpretation of normal remuneration before §4.1.1. Numeric equality TO = TK may remain familiar break-even arithmetic, not a long-run conclusion. Preserve independent consolidation before the Book 3 assessment.

**v3 implementation:** Substantive assessment-boundary repair is required: current exercises 35, 36e–f, 37 and the third source use long-run theory. Reuse valid short-run components; map relocated long-run evidence to Book 4 rather than deleting its Year 1 goal.

## Chapter 3.3 — Internationale handel

**Organising question:** Why do countries trade, who is affected, and how should we assess protection?

Retain three theory/application lessons and one mixed lesson. Trade closes Book 3 as a conceptual/source-led application of familiar markets. Keep qualitative comparative advantage, the world-price model, import/export gaps and bounded tariff/quota reasoning together.

**Entry prerequisites:** Book 1 scarcity/opportunity-cost meaning, demand/supply and source reading; Book 2 surplus; Chapter 3.1 tax/budget/quota meanings and §3.2.1 competitive price taking. Long-run firm adjustment is not needed for the core trade targets. The delivered closing review in §3.3.4 nevertheless contains a long-run question (38b); that concrete dependency must be repaired.

**Mathematical limits:** no calculated comparative-advantage algorithm, specialisation ratios, exchange-ratio bounds, balance-of-payments model, variable exchange rates or formal labour-productivity prerequisite. Retain one manageable familiar numerical chain per theory target with substantive interpretation. Do not turn the chapter into either a vocabulary checklist or a second optimisation chapter.

### 3.3.1 Waarom landen handelen: specialisatie en concurrentiepositie

**Role:** Conceptual theory and source-based application.

**Intended learning:** Explain specialisation, absolute versus comparative advantage and possible mutual gains qualitatively. Distinguish an explanation of relative opportunity cost from a general claim about a country's production strengths or competitiveness.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve scarcity, choice and the meaning of opportunity cost from Book 1. Introduce import/export and the new comparative-advantage meaning explicitly. Keep discussion of productivity, quality or costs qualitative and supported by information in the source; no formal labour-cost formula is assumed.

**Target design brief:** Use a short qualitative two-country case, including one in which the same country is more productive in both activities. Explain why relative advantage matters and how exchange may benefit both. Assess a short claim that cheaper production or greater productivity necessarily makes every participant better off. Require a reasoned answer, not a numerical specialisation table.

**Visual teaching plan:** A simple goods-flow sketch and an annotated qualitative relative-strength comparison. No production-capacity table that implicitly asks students to calculate a specialisation solution.

**Balance choice:** The target is explanation-led, with no required numerical comparative-advantage calculation. A compact source supplies the facts needed for the reasoning; students need not import labour-market knowledge from Book 4.

**Boundary and review focus:** No calculated opportunity-cost ratios, production-possibility-frontier optimisation, exchange-ratio bounds or separate ruilvoet calculation objective. Do not equate absolute with comparative advantage or claim that all groups necessarily gain. The conceptual/no-calculation boundary follows the owner's retained design instruction; this revision makes no new CE/SE validation claim. Formal productivity and unit-labour-cost analysis is taught in §4.3.1. Long-run entry/exit is not needed for this trade target.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the delivered teaching and target by default; update only affected references and any separately justified repair.

### 3.3.2 Wereldmarktprijs, import, export en welvaart

**Role:** Conceptual market interpretation with bounded numerical transfer.

**Intended learning:** Explain how a stated world price changes domestic production and consumption; identify whether the country imports or exports; calculate the trade gap and interpret the effects on domestic buyers and sellers.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse reading a demand/supply graph, determining quantity at a stated price, and the meaning of consumer/producer surplus. The new distinction is between domestic production, domestic consumption and the foreign trade flow, not a new equation-solving system.

**Target design brief:** Provide a labelled domestic market graph or a short table and a world price. Read or calculate domestic supply and demand, determine the import/export gap, and explain a buyer and seller outcome with evidence. Ask one qualitative surplus/distribution question. Include the reversed-price/export case in accessible practice without a second long calculation chain.

**Visual teaching plan:** A world-price line through familiar domestic demand and supply. Mark domestic production, domestic consumption and the gap separately. Use simple shading to support surplus interpretation rather than automatically requiring all areas to be calculated.

**Balance choice:** One short numerical chain supports the explanation. If algebra is selected, use familiar linear relations and easy values; do not simultaneously require full graph construction and multiple numerical welfare comparisons.

**Boundary and review focus:** State a small, price-taking country, competitive markets, sufficient foreign supply/demand and the relevant transport/tariff assumptions. Keep currencies fixed. Domestic demand is not domestic production. A rise in the stated total-surplus measure is not evidence that every group gains. The target need not produce a full welfare-area ledger or balance-of-payments account.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the delivered teaching and target by default; update only affected references and any separately justified repair.

### 3.3.3 Protectionisme: invoerheffingen en importquota

**Role:** Policy explanation with one bounded tariff application.

**Intended learning:** Explain why a government might restrict imports and distinguish its stated aim from effects on different groups. Use the familiar market model to interpret a per-unit import tariff; calculate remaining imports and tariff revenue in a simple case. Contrast an import quota qualitatively.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse §3.3.2's world-price model and Chapter 3.1's tax, government-budget and quota concepts. Explicitly distinguish a tariff on imported units from a tax on all domestic sales.

**Target design brief:** Present one short policy source and a supplied before/after graph or quantity table. Determine the remaining imported quantity and tariff receipts, then explain effects on domestic consumers and producers. Evaluate one bounded argument for the policy. Use a short non-calculation contrast to explain why a quota need not create the same government revenue.

**Visual teaching plan:** Before/after price lines on the same domestic market graph; mark the import gap and a single tariff-revenue rectangle. A small stakeholder comparison supports reasoning without substituting labels for explanation.

**Balance choice:** Keep the numerical target to one familiar chain. Do not routinely require a new-equilibrium derivation, four surplus calculations, two welfare-loss triangles and a quota-allocation calculation in the same lesson. Quantitative welfare repetition can be selected later where it is already taught and fits the target, but is not the default gate for this paragraph.

**Boundary and review focus:** Use imported quantity, not all domestic sales, for tariff revenue. Teach the no-import boundary using graph reading or a supplied contrast; do not mechanically impose world price plus tariff after imports disappear. State the model assumptions behind the case. Quantitative quota rents/allocation, export-subsidy models, exchange-rate adjustment and formal strategic retaliation remain deferred. An industry-gain claim is not automatically an economy-wide welfare claim.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the delivered teaching and target by default; update only affected references and any separately justified repair.

### 3.3.4 Gemengde opgaven: internationale handel

**Role:** Consolidation; no new theory.

**Intended learning:** Select and combine qualitative trade explanation, familiar world-price interpretation, a short import/tariff calculation and a source-supported conclusion. Distinguish aggregate and distributional claims.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve §§3.3.1-3.3.3 and already-taught market, budget and surplus meanings. No monopoly or labour-market procedure is needed. A source may mention jobs in ordinary language, but must not require labour-demand equations, participation statistics or unit-labour-cost calculations.

**Target design brief:** Use one compact trade-policy case plus a short contrasting claim. Students select the relevant evidence, complete one manageable numerical chain from familiar graph/table data, explain effects on different groups and give a qualified conclusion. Comparative-advantage reasoning stays verbal. Supply or directly teach any contextual information not already in the course.

**Visual teaching plan:** A familiar domestic market graph and a short source or stakeholder table; no new diagram family is introduced in consolidation.

**Balance choice:** This is a reasoning-led capstone, not a disguised return to a long calculation-heavy exam paper. Keep the support faded to independent source interpretation while maintaining manageable reading load and explicit question wording.

**Boundary and review focus:** The earlier proposal cites a 2022 VWO-I Importheffing task as a possible target-design input. Carry that reference forward for later source verification, not as a completed adaptation or evidence that every operation is approved. Later current-account and game-theory demands stay outside this chapter. Claims about protected jobs do not require or establish a formal economy-wide employment result.

**v3 implementation:** Retain the trade target and its conceptual/numerical boundaries. Replace or relocate current closing review 38b, which asks for long-run entry/price adjustment; rewrite the forward pointer so Book 4 first completes long-run competition, then introduces monopoly.
