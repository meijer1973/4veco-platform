## Shared authoring contract


Use backward design: proposed goals -> reviewed target -> observable operations -> explanation, worked example and practice. Every required operation must be explicitly taught or demonstrably retrieved; a topic encountered only as a preview is not secure prior learning.

Preserve the inherited Book 2+ printed exercise sequence: **Uitgewerkt voorbeeld; Startopgaven; Begeleide inoefening; Zelfstandige oefening; Doeloefening; Denkertje / Bonusopgave; Herhaling / Herhaling en interleaving**. Guided practice is a printed support option, not an extra destination. The core is Startopgaven -> Zelfstandige oefening -> Doeloefening. A teacher may assign the prerequisite part of Startopgaven at the beginning of the lesson without rearranging the printed headings. Mixed-exercise paragraphs are consolidation lessons: do not force a new-theory template onto them.

Plan a complete 55-minute lesson, including instruction and the worked example, not just student exercise time. Use 3-5 minutes of relevant prerequisite retrieval within the compact start work, then make the previous technique necessary again in the current worked example, independent practice and target. Closing review supplies spaced practice in other already-taught operations. Do not turn all seven printed sections into a compulsory one-lesson workload.

First transfer to an unfamiliar context requires a short explicit bridge: identify the actors, rename the variables, preserve the calculation steps, explain the changed interpretation, then fade the support. New economic meaning must not be disguised as mere revision.

All essential material remains paper-first and independent of devices. Use Dutch suitable for 4 VWO, established economic terminology and short source-specific conclusions. Use 'productiecapaciteit', not newly coined student-facing substitutes. A blank table must have an explicit completion instruction before questions refer to its results. Give every graph axis a variable, unit and usable scale; distinguish a market graph from a firm graph. Plan visuals to support actual target operations, not as decoration.

Inherited working print assumption for later production: carry forward the Book 2 ceiling of 40 pages per chapter for explanation plus exercises, excluding answers. This proposal is not a rendered-page feasibility certificate. The longer chapters require early page and timing trials; do not recover space through unreadably small type or by deleting necessary practice. Test preparation is not a separate printed chapter.

## Book-level decision

**Proposed title: Boek 4 — Monopolie, marktfalen en arbeidsmarkt.**

Retain the expanded **17 count-bearing paragraphs**, now allocated **4 + 7 + 6**. Start with monopoly, moved from the previous proposal's Chapter 3.3. The previous Chapter 4.1 becomes 4.2; the previous six-paragraph labour chapter 4.2 becomes 4.3. International trade now closes Book 3 and is no longer a Book 4 chapter.

The learning rhythm is **the monopolist's decision -> welfare, market failure and policy -> transfer to the labour market**. Monopoly analysis and its welfare consequences now occur consecutively within the same book. The labour chapter remains an explicit translation of familiar methods, not a second introduction to all market mathematics.

At book exit, students should select and use the correct monopoly procedure, evaluate market power and external effects using stated welfare assumptions, and translate market analysis into wage, employment and labour-policy contexts. Previously taught techniques must reappear in the worked example and target, not only in optional review.

## Chapter 4.1 — Monopolie

**Organising question:** What changes when the firm faces the market demand curve?

**Purpose:** Open Book 4 by reactivating the competitive-firm procedure from Chapter 3.2, then change the demand and revenue structure. The intervening trade chapter makes retrieval explicit rather than optional. This chapter supplies the monopoly outcome which Chapter 4.2 immediately evaluates in welfare terms.

**Entry prerequisites:** Chapter 3.2 supplies output optimisation; Book 2 supplies costs, revenue, profit and demand responsiveness. Retrieve market-versus-firm graphs in §4.1.1, TO and the limited derivative operation in §4.1.2, and marginal output choice in §4.1.3. Do not assume uninterrupted fluency merely because these were taught in Book 3. Welfare comparison is reserved for §4.2.1.

**Book-opening bridge:** Use the prerequisite part of the existing Startopgaven for a short competitive-firm question with P = GO = MO, a cost relation and a familiar output choice. The teacher may assign it before instruction. This is not an additional count-bearing paragraph, an extra prerequisite chapter or a reason to repeat all of Chapter 3.2.

### 4.1.1 Monopolie: kenmerken

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Explain market power, barriers to entry and the relationship between the firm’s sales and market demand.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Contrast with §3.2.1; retrieve the demand function and price/quantity relationship. Use the book-opening retrieval task to restore the difference between a market graph and the competitive firm's graph before introducing the monopolist's graph.

**Proposed target brief:** Use a source to identify the barrier to entry, distinguish monopoly from perfect competition and explain the price/quantity trade-off.

**Visual teaching plan:** Competitive and monopolist firm demand diagrams with matching actor labels.

**Boundary and review focus:** Monopoly does not mean unlimited pricing power. Patents can be one entry-barrier context; full policy evaluation belongs later.

### 4.1.2 Marginale opbrengst bij monopolie

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Explain why extra sales and a lower price have different effects on total revenue; obtain MO from a simple TO relation.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve TO = P x Q, Book 2 marginal differences and revenue interpretation, and the limited derivative technique taught in §3.2.2.

**Proposed target brief:** Move from a linear demand function to TO and MO, check the result against a short table, and explain why MO differs from the selling price.

**Visual teaching plan:** A revenue rectangle split into the gain on extra units and the reduction on existing units; aligned demand/MO curves.

**Boundary and review focus:** Assume a uniform selling price. Do not introduce point-elasticity formulas or a general calculus chapter. Revisit elasticity qualitatively only where it supports the taught revenue interpretation.

### 4.1.3 Winstmaximalisatie bij monopolie

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Determine output using MO and MK, then read price from demand and calculate profit.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Repeat §3.2.2’s optimisation procedure, §4.1.2’s revenue relation and Book 2 cost calculations.

**Proposed target brief:** Calculate the feasible monopoly output, corresponding selling price and total profit, then reject a common wrong-method claim using the calculation.

**Visual teaching plan:** A numbered visual route: MO = MK -> quantity -> demand/GO -> price -> profit.

**Boundary and review focus:** Do not read price from the MO curve, equate P with MK at the monopoly optimum, or confuse maximum revenue with maximum profit. Reserve the formal welfare comparison for §4.2.1, immediately after this chapter; it is not postponed to a later book.

### 4.1.4 Gemengde opgaven: monopolie

**Role:** Consolidation; no new theory.

**Intended learning:** Choose and execute the monopoly calculation sequence and compare it with the already-taught competitive firm sequence.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Interleave cost, revenue, elasticity interpretation, break-even and marginal choice without adding new operations.

**Proposed target brief:** Use a monopoly case with sources and a compact competitive contrast; explain why the correct price-reading step differs.

**Visual teaching plan:** A firm graph plus a cost/revenue source; students supply the missing labels or steps required by the target.

**Boundary and review focus:** No price discrimination, strategic game or monopoly-welfare target before those are taught. Consolidation is not an extra theory paragraph.

## Chapter 4.2 — Marktvormen en marktfalen

**Organising question:** When does the market outcome fail to maximise the relevant welfare measure, and what can policy change?

**Purpose:** Make the methods from Books 1-3 necessary again. The chapter first evaluates market power, then introduces third-party effects and revisits taxes and subsidies under the changed welfare assumptions.

**Entry prerequisites:** Chapter 4.1 supplies monopoly output and price; Chapter 3.2 supplies competitive-firm analysis; Chapter 3.1 supplies intervention methods. Book 2 supplies costs, revenue, elasticity and surplus; Book 1 supplies functions, equilibrium, shifts, percentages and source reading. Labour-market analysis in Chapter 4.3 is not a prerequisite.

### 4.2.1 Welvaartseffecten van monopolie

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Compare monopoly with an appropriate competitive/efficient benchmark and distinguish a redistribution of surplus from a welfare loss.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Repeat §4.1.3’s quantity-and-price calculation and Book 2 CS/PS/total-surplus areas as essential parts of the current task.

**Proposed target brief:** Determine both benchmark quantities, calculate or identify the surplus distribution and lost gains from trade, and explain why higher producer returns are not identical to higher total welfare.

**Visual teaching plan:** One demand/MO/MK diagram with both outcomes and a separate explanation of each shaded area.

**Boundary and review focus:** Keep demand, cost conditions and welfare boundary comparable across scenarios. Producer surplus is not automatically profit; do not treat every transfer between buyer and seller as a loss.

### 4.2.2 Prijsdiscriminatie

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Explain the conditions for selling at different prices and analyse the effect on revenue, profit and the division of surplus.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse TO, profit and demand responsiveness; where the chosen target requires it, repeat the established MO/MK procedure in each explicitly separated market.

**Proposed target brief:** Compare uniform pricing with a bounded two-group pricing case using supplied demand/cost evidence; explain one feasibility condition and one distributional effect.

**Visual teaching plan:** Two clearly separated demand panels or a two-group source table; a comparison of price, sales and surplus.

**Boundary and review focus:** State market separation and resale restrictions. For separate segment optimisation, use a stated common constant marginal cost, or explicitly account for costs that depend on combined output. Do not claim that discrimination necessarily raises or necessarily lowers total welfare. Avoid adding simultaneous unfamiliar cost structures and multiple optimisation cases.

### 4.2.3 Marktvormen vergelijken

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Distinguish perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly and monopoly; transfer a familiar profit procedure to a supplied unfamiliar-market representation.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse competitive/monopoly reasoning, substitute relationships, demand responsiveness and marginal profit choice.

**Proposed target brief:** Classify firms from concise sources and apply the already-taught calculation to one appropriate supplied firm diagram or function; justify the classification and outcome.

**Visual teaching plan:** A compact comparison grid and one carefully selected firm-graph transfer.

**Boundary and review focus:** Do not assume an oligopolist has one universal demand curve. No reaction functions or payoff-matrix techniques here. Formal strategic interaction stays in the later course. This paragraph is not just a vocabulary checklist, but it also must not become four full new models.

### 4.2.4 Negatieve externe effecten

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Identify unpriced harm to third parties, distinguish private from social consequences and analyse a corrective tax.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Repeat the tax calculation from §§3.1.1-3.1.2, the market equilibrium method and surplus accounting. The external-cost interpretation is new.

**Proposed target brief:** Identify the affected third party, compare the unregulated and taxed outcome, calculate the taught market/budget consequences and use supplied external-cost evidence to evaluate welfare.

**Visual teaching plan:** A private/social-cost comparison linked explicitly to the familiar tax-wedge diagram; introduce each new line before using it.

**Boundary and review focus:** Do not count tax revenue as a real social loss or treat CS + PS alone as the full welfare measure when external harm is present. Keep external-cost assumptions explicit and simple; no unsupported claims of a uniquely optimal tax.

### 4.2.5 Positieve externe effecten

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Identify benefits to third parties and explain how a subsidy can improve an outcome with too little of the beneficial activity.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Repeat §3.1.3’s subsidy wedge, quantity and government-expenditure calculation and Book 2 surplus reasoning.

**Proposed target brief:** Use an illustrative activity with a stated external benefit and subsidy rule. Calculate the new market outcome and public spending, then explain why the welfare conclusion differs from a subsidy without an external benefit.

**Visual teaching plan:** A labelled external-benefit diagram connected to the same subsidy representation already used in Book 3.

**Boundary and review focus:** Keep the benefit to the buyer separate from the benefit to others. State who receives the subsidy. Do not count a transfer twice or assume every subsidy is appropriately sized. The external-benefit interpretation is new teaching, not prerequisite recall.

### 4.2.6 Overheidsingrijpen bij marktfalen

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Match a policy to the diagnosed failure, compare feasible alternatives and evaluate effects and limitations with evidence.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse taxes, subsidies, price restrictions and quantity restrictions, alongside monopoly and externality analysis.

**Proposed target brief:** Compare two policy proposals for one clearly described problem. Carry out one familiar calculation and use an explicit criterion to justify a qualified recommendation.

**Visual teaching plan:** A problem -> mechanism -> instrument -> outcome comparison, with a small numerical or graphical source.

**Boundary and review focus:** Not a second full course on every instrument. A patent/competition-policy example can retrieve market power; avoid importing the later games, insurance or macro-policy curriculum. Separate efficiency from distribution and recognise implementation limits stated in the source.

### 4.2.7 Gemengde opgaven: marktvormen en marktfalen

**Role:** Consolidation; no new theory.

**Intended learning:** Integrate firm behaviour, surplus, external effects and policy selection without new theory.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Select useful methods from Books 1-3, Chapter 4.1 and §§4.2.1-4.2.6, rather than repeating a labelled routine mechanically.

**Proposed target brief:** Use contrasting short cases: at least one market-power case and one externality/policy case. The target requires numerical or graphical evidence and a bounded economic conclusion.

**Visual teaching plan:** Previously introduced diagram families with less scaffolding; make the required model choice visible in the answer model.

**Boundary and review focus:** Do not put an unfamiliar policy or welfare concept into the mixed target. Keep core work feasible; distribute additional retrieval to the closing review.

## Chapter 4.3 — Arbeidsmarkt

**Organising question:** How do familiar market methods work when the price is a wage and households supply the factor of production?

**Purpose:** The registry-baseline standalone equilibrium paragraph (§4.2.3 in the old blueprint) is absorbed, not erased. Teach the actor/variable translation explicitly, practise baseline equilibrium in §4.3.2, revisit shifts in §4.3.3, and use the same model in §4.3.4 and §4.3.6.

**Entry prerequisites:** Books 1-3: demand/supply, equilibrium, shifts, price floors, costs and percentage/index calculations. Chapter 3.3 now also supplies qualitative trade and domestic-market context. Labour-market terminology, participation, productivity and unemployment classifications still require explicit teaching here. Trade must not already have required the labour-productivity or unit-labour-cost calculations introduced below.

### 4.3.1 Arbeidsvraag en arbeidsproductiviteit

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Explain why employers demand labour, interpret changes in that demand, and relate output, labour input, productivity and labour cost per unit.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve demand shifts, average-cost reasoning, ratios and percentages. Re-label price as wage and show that employers are the buyers of labour.

**Proposed target brief:** Use a firm or sector source to calculate productivity or required labour and labour cost per product, then explain a movement along or shift of labour demand.

**Visual teaching plan:** A goods-market/labour-market comparison plus a labelled output -> labour-input calculation table. A short source about export orders or import competition can retrieve Chapter 3.3 while changing only the labour-demand interpretation.

**Boundary and review focus:** Keep hours, persons and full-time equivalents distinct. Do not imply productivity growth always reduces total employment; state output and other conditions. No formal marginal-revenue-product optimisation unless separately justified and taught. This is the first formal home of the productivity and unit-labour-cost calculations; revisit a qualitative competitiveness claim from §3.3.1 only after teaching the needed calculation here.

### 4.3.2 Arbeidsaanbod, participatie en evenwicht

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Identify households as labour suppliers, calculate participation with the stated denominator, and find and interpret the competitive baseline equilibrium wage and employment.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse Book 1 equilibrium equations and graph reading. A short explicit translation bridge precedes one compact equilibrium calculation; the algebra is not retaught as new.

**Proposed target brief:** Read a population/participation source and a separate simple market model. Calculate the specified participation measure, then equilibrium wage and quantity, and explain who supplies and who demands labour.

**Visual teaching plan:** A small population composition scheme and paired generic-market/labour-market graphs with identical mathematical structure.

**Boundary and review focus:** This paragraph absorbs the baseline part of the registry-baseline §4.2.3, not an entire extra lesson hidden inside it. Keep participation data simple, units fixed and the equilibrium model familiar. Distinguish a supply curve at different wages from measured labour-force totals. Review timing before finalising the target.

### 4.3.3 Werkloosheid en veranderingen op de arbeidsmarkt

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Calculate unemployment using the labour force as denominator, distinguish the relevant causes and interpret labour-market changes.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Reuse §4.3.2’s equilibrium and Book 1 shift/new-equilibrium method. This is the second home of the absorbed equilibrium work.

**Proposed target brief:** Combine a compact labour-market source with one shift scenario: calculate unemployment, identify the cause and explain the direction of wage/employment pressure under stated adjustment assumptions.

**Visual teaching plan:** A labour-force composition table alongside one before/after labour-market graph.

**Boundary and review focus:** Distinguish the simplified clearing model from observed vacancies and mismatch. With data defined as labour demand = employment + vacancies, supply minus demand is not measured unemployment. Explain structural/frictional and demand-related unemployment at an introductory level; no output-gap or Phillips-curve model.

### 4.3.4 Minimumloon

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Apply a minimum-price model to wages and distinguish effects on wage rates, employment, people seeking work and the wage bill.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Repeat §3.1.5’s price-floor technique and §4.3.2’s labour-market translation; reuse price-times-quantity and percentage calculations.

**Proposed target brief:** Determine whether a minimum wage binds, calculate the taught model outcomes and wage bill, and explain the difference between workers who retain employment and those who do not obtain work.

**Visual teaching plan:** A price-floor/minimum-wage pair with the same analytical steps and changed labels.

**Boundary and review focus:** State a competitive model, the wage unit and adjustment assumptions. Model outcomes are not universal empirical claims about every minimum-wage change. Use illustrative rates, not unverified current statutory figures.

### 4.3.5 Vakbonden, cao en arbeidsmarktbeleid

**Role:** Theory and application.

**Intended learning:** Explain collective bargaining and assess a bounded policy or agreement using wages, productivity, labour supply/demand and employment.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Repeat percentage/index comparisons, labour cost per unit, shifts and the difference between a binding wage floor and a change in the underlying market.

**Proposed target brief:** Evaluate a concise collective-agreement or training-policy proposal. Support the conclusion with a familiar calculation and a clearly stated mechanism.

**Visual teaching plan:** A short policy/source comparison with one wage/productivity table or labour-market diagram.

**Boundary and review focus:** Do not turn this into formal bargaining-game theory. Introduce flexwork/zzp terminology as needed for the source without assuming that labels determine effects. Use institutional descriptions rather than unsupported current legal details. A competitiveness application may now combine already-taught trade reasoning with wage/productivity comparisons; it does not retrospectively make those calculations a Book 3 prerequisite.

### 4.3.6 Gemengde opgaven: arbeidsmarkt

**Role:** Consolidation; no new theory.

**Intended learning:** Integrate labour-market vocabulary, equilibrium and shifts, participation/unemployment, productivity and policy without new theory.

**Retrieval and transfer:** Retrieve all chapter operations plus the earlier minimum-price method. Include independent equilibrium evidence explicitly so it has not disappeared with the removed paragraph.

**Proposed target brief:** Use manageable source-based cases with at least one independent wage/quantity calculation and one qualified employment or policy conclusion.

**Visual teaching plan:** A population table, a wage/quantity graph and a short policy source; do not overload a single exercise with every possible operation.

**Boundary and review focus:** No seventh compulsory catch-up lesson. If the absorbed equilibrium targets cannot fit the existing lessons and supports, revise the exercise design and timing before production rather than claiming the capacity saving without evidence.
