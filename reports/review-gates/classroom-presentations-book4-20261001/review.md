# Book 4 classroom presentations: batch and teaching-sequence review

**PASS for the selected delivered artifacts and reviewed teaching sequence.**
The delivery contains all 17 current-v3 paragraphs, 438 slides, 17 editable
PowerPoint files and 17 matching slide PDFs. Each deck includes teacher notes,
complete assigned target sources/questions and an authored explanation example.
Builders, source manifests and individual evidence accompany the outputs.
This review is not merge authority or evidence of measured classroom outcomes.

## What the production experiment establishes

Every original author received exactly `Build the PowerPoint for paragraph X.`
in a fresh ephemeral CLI thread, with no parent conversation, paragraph answer
plan, sibling presentation or coordinator feedback. Seventeen separate paired
worktrees began at platform `7e258769e98cf4c7bee46fe3a7fe8673b3696aba` and
lessons `e734532a42b27732ac25ce990fc9448b12309d28`, with up to three authors
working concurrently. The original user-configured model settings were retained.
Authors could use the repository, installed artifact tools, their own review
agents and their own corrections before handing off.

Before production, an independently reviewed preparation change documented
Book 4's actual v3 source/output locations and admitted its bounded classroom
additions to the sealed-edition check. It supplied no new Book 4 teaching content.
The Book 3 pair was already merged; its final post-merge CI retry completed
during early Book 4 production, as recorded in the merge-prerequisite evidence.

The worktrees shared a host filesystem. The exposed-command scan and manual
dispositions record observed dependency lookups and any cross-worktree paths;
they are not an operating-system isolation guarantee. Original output commits,
prompt records and log hashes are retained separately from selected replacements
and coordinator repairs.

The result supports parallel production with review and repair. It does not
establish flawless first drafts or justify removing the final native-render
review. Autonomous author revisions count as part of a cold run; coordinator
interventions after the completed handoff are identified explicitly.

## Workflow repairs and fresh tests

The original batch exposed three recurring presentation risks: chapter-local
page numbers presented as complete-book references, direct graph labels touching
curves, and a claim that a model's supply curve was arbitrary despite linked
firm and market equations fixing it. The recipe now requires every page anchor
to point to actual teaching in the right edition, each repeated/panelled chart
label to have visible clearance, and linked representations to agree with the
same costs, firm count and capacity assumptions.

Those general instruction changes were independently reviewed at platform
`d1cb1fe3db3a70dc86682491771ed68a67f45526`. Fresh §4.1.1 and §4.2.2 authors
received the same minimal paragraph prompts in new paired worktrees. Their
seed contained no newly authored Book 4 builder, deck, answer plan or prior
findings. Original histories remain frozen.

Both completed fresh artifacts pass independent review and replace their original
selections: §4.1.1 has 28 slides and §4.2.2 has 27. The fresh authors autonomously
corrected graph labels during their own review, before handing off. The §4.2.2
author also clarified a profit/surplus sentence and made both start items’ book
support visible. These intermediate candidate defects remain recorded; neither
completed selected deck required coordinator content or visual repair.

The §4.1.1 retest explicitly derives aggregate supply from the same firm costs,
firm counts and capacity in both entry and exit cases. It also independently
identified the source figure’s out-of-capacity extension, bounded its answer
graph and documented the caveat. The §4.2.2 retest uses actual complete-book
pages 36–38 for its prerequisite methods and separates the group pricing,

## The serialized teaching narrative

The review traced actual operations in the earlier Book 2/3 presentations and
the current textbook. A prerequisite name alone was not treated as evidence
that students had been taught it. Independent foundation and chapter reviews
record their own source, mathematical and visual scope separately.

**4.1: from a price taker to a price-setting firm.** The first lesson retrieves
firm/market units, derivatives, feasible marginal choices and profit areas from
Book 3, then explicitly introduces economic profit, entry/exit and long-run
equilibrium. Normal remuneration is already in costs. The next lesson changes
the market assumptions: a sole seller faces its own falling demand, and cannot
choose price and quantity independently. GO=P still holds under one uniform
price, while MO=P no longer follows.

The revenue lesson teaches the two effects of a price reduction before deriving
MO: revenue on additional sales and lower revenue on the previously sold units
in the alternative plan. Interval averages and point derivatives remain distinct.
The optimization lesson then combines MO and MK, checks direction, capacity and
zero production, and reads the sale price from demand at that same quantity.
MO=0 is a revenue maximum; MO=MK is a profit candidate. Fixed-cost and demand
changes are separate scenarios. Mixed practice retains actual exercise 41A and
does not infer welfare rankings from the profits of two different market models.

**4.2: private choices and social consequences.** Monopoly welfare first separates
producer surplus from profit, transfers from lost surplus, and private optimum
from efficient quantity under the stated no-externality model. Price discrimination
then changes the pricing assumption explicitly: separated groups, no resale,
common constant marginal cost and sufficient capacity. Fixed costs are deducted
once. A producer gain does not prove a total-surplus gain, even at equal total
output. The market-form comparison resets uniform pricing and teaches that
product differentiation can give a falling firm-demand curve without monopoly;
it does not invent a universal oligopoly demand equation.

Negative external effects add unpaid third-party costs to the social marginal
comparison. The familiar tax wedge now operates within a different welfare
ledger: public receipts are included, remaining harm is deducted, and receipt
does not imply victim compensation. Positive external effects add third-party
benefits and subtract subsidy spending on all subsidized sales. A partial-subsidy
variant demonstrates that spending and benefits need not be numerically equal.
The same subsidy is also compared with a no-external-benefit case, explaining why
its welfare effect differs from Book 3 without contradicting it.

Policy comparison adds implementation costs as real resource use and distinguishes
efficiency, distribution and public budget. The tax/quota comparison states free
permits, efficient allocation and compliance assumptions rather than hiding them.
Mixed practice asks students to select the appropriate model for two complete
source cases. The monopoly case uses a week and the external-cost case a day;
their quantities and totals are never added. An intervention appropriate to one
market is not automatically prescribed for the other.

The labour-market chapter starts by teaching the units it needs: persons, paid
hours, fte, output per hour and full labour cost per product. It then separates
regional workforce counts from a sector model and establishes the distinct
denominators for participation and unemployment. Vacancies are unfilled places;
subtracting vacancies from unemployed people does not produce unemployment.

A demand shock is compared under flexible and then fixed wages. The minimum-wage
lesson subsequently tests whether a floor binds before calculating employment
and excess supply, and explicitly teaches payroll before independent practice.
It separates people losing work from new labour suppliers and retained individual
pay from the total wage bill. Hours per worker change from the own example to the
target only when the new source says so. A minimum wage does not create automatic
government hiring.

The final mixed lesson retrieves the preceding methods and keeps its three sources
separate: regional population, a sector with flexible wages, and an individual
company. Extra orders cause the demand shift; the higher wage is a result and
causes movement along unchanged supply. Unit labour costs use the ratio of growth
factors, with an unrounded intermediate value. Lower unit costs do not guarantee
more jobs when future orders and hours per worker are unknown. No missing teaching
prerequisite or contradictory model transition was found in this sequence.

New-content starts are supported explorations with visible reading support and
a return after explanation, before independent practice. Full target contexts,
conditions, figures/tables and every subquestion appear before answers. Authored
teaching examples use different contexts/data from assigned practice. Mixed
paragraphs preserve the first actual exercise and complete real homework roles.

## Textbook foundation and limits

The independent source audit covered all 17 current-edition manuscripts, all
three chapter answer sets and teacher guides, surrounding summaries and selected
high-risk printed pages. The presentation review found no blocking mathematical
or prerequisite defect in that foundation. The records identify four separate
editorial follow-ups: the §4.1.1 market-supply continuation exceeds aggregate
capacity outside the assessed range; a §4.3.3 answer points its TO/MO remediation
to §4.1.2 instead of §4.1.3; and four labour-market source figures extend outside
their stated wage domains; and the final mixed answer blocks retain stale outer
exercise IDs while their visible numbers and per-part keys are correct. Relevant final presentations respect the valid ranges
and actual teaching anchors. In §4.3.5 the original source figure remains visible
with a domain warning, followed by a bounded editable answer graph.

An author's provisional concern about restarted page numbers in §4.3.5 was
resolved by inspecting individual pages and PDF text geometry. The printed page
numbers are correct; this is not a textbook finding. See the source follow-up
record for the precise locations and limits of the genuine editorial findings.

No textbook source, answer model, teacher guide, student PDF or sealed receipt
was edited. Source follow-ups remain distinct from presentation conversion
repairs. The earlier Book 3 glossary/heading-marker follow-ups also remain outside
this delivery. Two 55-minute lessons in the guide are provisional guidance, not
a measured duration. This review does not certify target candidates formally or
establish pupil mastery, learning effectiveness or whole companion-product
completeness.
