## Book 9: Keynesian Macro And Fiscal Policy

Book 9 is the first Year 3 test-week book. It moves from aggregate demand and
Keynesian expenditure logic to output gaps, automatic stabilizers, fiscal
policy choices, debt/budget constraints, and open-economy leakage. It retrieves
Book 8 public finance and growth bridge content but does not absorb detailed
monetary transmission.

Chapters:

| Chapter | Focus | Candidate range | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9.1 | Aggregate expenditure, multiplier, and effective demand | `Y3-B9-P01` to `Y3-B9-P04` | Build Keynesian demand mechanics. |
| 9.2 | Output gaps, shocks, and stabilization | `Y3-B9-P05` to `Y3-B9-P08` | Connect model state to fiscal-policy choice. |
| 9.3 | Fiscal policy, budget balance, and debt | `Y3-B9-P09` to `Y3-B9-P12` | Tie stabilization to public-finance constraints. |
| 9.4 | Fiscal source cases and Book 9 synthesis | `Y3-B9-P13` to `Y3-B9-P14` | Practise fiscal/output-gap evidence and cumulative retrieval. |

Count model: 14 count-bearing candidates. This stays inside the v6 12-16
envelope.

## Book 10: Monetary Policy, IS-MB-GA, And Open Economy

Book 10 is the second Year 3 test-week book. It takes the macro state from Book
9 and adds monetary transmission, IS-MB-GA representation, central-bank policy,
exchange-rate channels, fixed-rate constraints, trilemma/open-economy trade-
offs, and policy-mix comparison. q23 from `Groen ontgroeien` belongs here as
partial official evidence for monetary-policy effectiveness.

Chapters:

| Chapter | Focus | Candidate range | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10.1 | Monetary-policy goals, interest-rate channel, and IS-MB-GA basics | `Y3-B10-P01` to `Y3-B10-P05` | Build transmission and model literacy. |
| 10.2 | Central-bank reaction and exchange-rate channel | `Y3-B10-P06` to `Y3-B10-P09` | Connect interest policy to inflation, output, and external value. |
| 10.3 | Open-economy constraints and policy mix | `Y3-B10-P10` to `Y3-B10-P12` | Add capital-flow, fixed-rate, and fiscal/monetary trade-offs. |
| 10.4 | Model drawing, schools, and synthesis | `Y3-B10-P13` to `Y3-B10-P15` | Practise curve shifts, adjustment mechanisms, and cumulative synthesis. |

Count model: 15 count-bearing candidates. This stays inside the v6 12-16
envelope.

Year 3 count summary: Book 9 = 14, Book 10 = 15.

## Book 11: Integrated Policy And Final Exam Training

Book 11 is not a fourth theory-heavy Year 3 book. It is the cumulative exam
training book. It introduces exam-operation modules, mixed retrieval cases, and
timed paper cycles, but timed papers do not automatically count as ordinary
textbook paragraphs.

Book 11 module model:

| Segment | Role | Candidate range | Count treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11A | Exam operation decomposition | `Y3-B11-M01` to `Y3-B11-M04` | count-bearing instructional/exam-operation modules |
| 11B | Mixed retrieval cases | `Y3-B11-M05` to `Y3-B11-M08` | count-bearing mixed retrieval cases |
| 11C | Timed full-paper cycles | `Y3-B11-M09`, `Y3-B11-M11` | timed paper cycles, not ordinary textbook paragraphs |
| 11D | Repair, retest, and final synthesis | `Y3-B11-M10`, `Y3-B11-M12`, `Y3-B11-M13` | repair/retest cycles plus count-bearing capstone module |

Working count model:

- 9 count-bearing modules: `M01-M08` and `M13`.
- 2 timed full-paper cycles: `M09`, `M11`; tracked as exam-practice cycles,
  not count-bearing paragraphs.
- 2 repair/retest cycles: `M10`, `M12`; tracked as feedback cycles.
- 13 total scheduled modules/cycles.

## Three-Year Boundary Review

| Boundary | Decision |
|---|---|
| Book 8 vs Book 9 | Book 8 keeps growth, green GDP, public finance, and strategy foundations; macro stabilization starts in Book 9. |
| Book 9 vs Book 10 | Book 9 may name interest and central banks only as fiscal-policy context; detailed monetary transmission and IS-MB-GA reaction belongs to Book 10. |
| Book 10 vs Book 11 | Book 10 teaches monetary/open-economy macro; timed full-paper practice and mixed correction-model discipline belong to Book 11. |
| Book 11 theory boundary | Book 11 integrates Books 1-10 and trains exam operations; it should not introduce large new theory domains. |
