## 2.1.4 Local Production Target Design

Status: local sprint design, not a mutation of `references/authored/course-target-exercises.json` and not final-reviewed target evidence yet.

Context: A small bakery is deciding whether to expand weekly bread production. Fixed costs are EUR 600 per week. For the first 800 loaves, variable cost is EUR 1.10 per loaf. Above 800 loaves, overtime raises the variable cost of each additional loaf to EUR 1.40. The bakery sells each loaf for EUR 2.00.

Target subquestions:

| Label | Student action |
|---|---|
| a | Write the formulas for `TCK`, `TVK`, and `TK` up to 800 loaves. Calculate `TK`, `GTK`, `GCK`, and `GVK` at `Q = 400` and `Q = 800`. |
| b | Write the formula for `TO`. Calculate `TO` and `winst` at `Q = 400` and `Q = 800`. |
| c | Calculate the break-even quantity for the first 800 loaves and explain what the result means. |
| d | Draw `TK` and `TO` for `Q = 0` through `Q = 800`, mark the break-even point, and shade the profit zone. |
| e | Complete a marginal table for `Q = 700`, `800`, `900`, and `1000` showing `TK`, `TO`, `winst`, `MK`, and `MO`. |
| f | Explain why `MK` changes after 800 loaves while `MO` stays the same. Use the table to advise whether producing from 800 to 900 loaves increases profit. |

Learning goals covered:

- Combine cost formulas, average costs, revenue, profit, and break-even.
- Interpret a break-even graph with `TK` and `TO`.
- Calculate `MK` and `MO` from table changes.
- Explain marginal reasoning without calculus.
- Transfer the chapter's earlier skills into one integrated exam-style context.
